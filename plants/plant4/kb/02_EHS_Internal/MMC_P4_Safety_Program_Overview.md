## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the Environmental, Health, and Safety (EHS) program framework for Midwest Mobility Components (MMC) Plant 4. It defines the management system, core safety programs, roles, responsibilities, and procedures for maintaining a safe work environment across all operations.

## 3. Safety Management System Framework

MMC Plant 4 operates an EHS management system aligned to **ISO 45001 principles**. The system follows the Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle:

### 3.1 System Elements

| Element | Description | Frequency |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| Policy & Leadership | Management commitment, resource allocation, safety objectives | Annual review |
| Hazard Identification | Proactive identification of workplace hazards | Ongoing |
| Risk Assessment | Evaluation of hazard severity and likelihood | Per hazard / change |
| Operational Controls | SOPs, engineering controls, administrative controls | Per task |
| Training & Competence | Role-based safety training and qualification | Initial + annual |
| Incident Management | Reporting, investigation, corrective action | Per event |
| Monitoring & Measurement | Inspections, audits, metrics tracking | Daily/weekly/monthly |
| Management Review | Leadership review of EHS performance | Quarterly |
| Continuous Improvement | Corrective actions, lessons learned, program updates | Ongoing |

### 3.2 Management Commitment Procedure
1. Plant Manager signs annual EHS Policy Statement
2. Leadership participates in monthly safety committee meetings
3. Plant Manager conducts quarterly shop floor safety walks
4. Safety performance is reviewed at weekly operations meetings
5. Resources are allocated for hazard correction and program improvement

## 5. Core Safety Programs

### 5.1 Lockout/Tagout (LOTO)

**Purpose:** Prevent unexpected energization during maintenance, servicing, and repair activities.

**Scope:** All equipment with hazardous energy sources including electrical, pneumatic, hydraulic, mechanical, thermal, chemical, and gravity.

| Energy Type | Common Sources at Plant 4 |
|-------------|---------------------------|
| Electrical | Motors, control panels, robot controllers, conveyor drives |
| Pneumatic | Air cylinders, press brakes, clamping systems |
| Hydraulic | Press hydraulics, lift tables, robot tooling |
| Mechanical | Flywheels, springs, counterweights, conveyor tension |
| Thermal | Heated dies, curing ovens, battery systems |
| Chemical | Process lines, cleaning systems, adhesive dispensers |
| Gravity | Elevated dies, suspended loads, lift tables |

**Key Procedures:**
1. Machine-specific LOTO procedures posted at each lockout point
2. Authorized employees apply personal locks and tags
3. Zero-energy verification required before work begins
4. Group lockout procedures for multi-craft maintenance
5. Shift transfer procedures for extended lockout

**Reference:** MMC_P4_LOTO_SOP

---

### 5.2 Machine Guarding

**Purpose:** Protect employees from point-of-operation hazards, pinch points, rotating parts, and flying debris.

**Scope:** All machinery on Lines 1, 2, and 3, plus support equipment.

| Line | Critical Guarding Points |
|------|--------------------------|
| Line 1 | Press point-of-operation, coil cradle, scrap conveyors |
| Line 2 | Robot cell perimeter, servo tool heads, indexing fixtures |
| Line 3 | Conveyor nip points, stretch-wrap spindle, labeler mechanisms |

**Key Procedures:**
1. Guards inspected during pre-shift equipment checks
2. Guard removal requires LOTO and supervisor authorization
3. Interlock bypass requires documented risk assessment and EHS approval
4. Temporary guards required during troubleshooting if original removed
5. Guard reinstallation verified before equipment restart

**Reference:** MMC_P4_Machine_Guarding_SOP

### 5.5 Powered Industrial Truck Safety

**Purpose:** Prevent injuries from forklift and pallet jack operations.

**Scope:** All forklifts, reach trucks, order pickers, and powered pallet jacks.

**Operating Areas:** Receiving/Shipping, internal aisles, staging areas

**Key Procedures:**
1. Only trained and certified operators may operate powered industrial trucks
2. Pre-shift inspection completed and documented before first use
3. Defective equipment tagged out and reported to Maintenance
4. Speed limits observed: 5 mph in aisles, 3 mph at intersections
5. Pedestrians have right-of-way; operators yield at all crossings
6. Loads secured and within rated capacity
7. Forks lowered and controls neutralized when parked

**Certification Requirements:**
| Element | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| Initial Training | Classroom + hands-on + evaluation |
| Practical Evaluation | Observed operation on actual equipment |
| Recertification | Every 3 years or after incident/unsafe operation |
| Refresher | After observed unsafe operation, near-miss, or equipment change |

### 5.6 Electrical Safety

**Purpose:** Protect employees from electrical shock, arc flash, and arc blast hazards.

**Scope:** All electrical systems, equipment, and maintenance activities.

**Key Procedures:**
1. Only qualified electrical workers perform energized work
2. De-energization is the default; energized work requires approved permit
3. Arc flash labels posted on all electrical panels
4. PPE selected based on incident energy analysis
5. Electrical rooms access restricted to authorized personnel

| Worker Classification | Permitted Activities |
|-----------------------|---------------------|
| Unqualified | Operate equipment per normal use; report hazards |
| Qualified | Install, maintain, troubleshoot electrical systems |
| Qualified (High Voltage) | Work on systems over 600V |

### 5.8 Elevated Work Surfaces and Fall Protection

**Purpose:** Prevent falls from elevated surfaces, platforms, ladders, and scaffolds during production, maintenance, and support activities.

**Scope:** All work performed at heights of 4 feet or more above a lower level in general industry areas.

**Elevated Work Locations at Plant 4:**

| Location | Height | Access Method | Fall Protection Required |
|----------|--------|---------------|-------------------------|
| Press mezzanine (Line 1) | 12 ft | Fixed stairs | Guardrails; harness for leaning over edge |
| Coil storage racks (Line 1) | 8-16 ft | Mobile ladder | Harness + lanyard |
| Robot cell tops (Line 2) | 10 ft | Fixed ladder with cage | Guardrails; harness for maintenance |
| Conveyor maintenance platforms (Line 3) | 6 ft | Fixed stairs | Guardrails |
| Shipping dock edge | 4 ft | Dock leveler | Dock chains; awareness barriers |
| Roof access | 20+ ft | Fixed ladder | Harness + roof anchor; permit required |
| Overhead crane maintenance | 15 ft | Fixed ladder | Harness + SRL at all times |
| Mezzanine storage areas | 10 ft | Fixed stairs | Guardrails; swing gates at load points |

**Fall Protection Methods (Hierarchy of Controls):**

| Priority | Control Type | Examples |
|----------|--------------|----------|
| 1 | Elimination | Perform work from ground level; use extension tools |
| 2 | Passive Fall Protection | Guardrails, safety nets, covers |
| 3 | Fall Restraint | Harness + lanyard limiting access to fall hazard |
| 4 | Fall Arrest | Harness + SRL or shock-absorbing lanyard + anchor |
| 5 | Administrative Controls | Warning lines, safety monitors, procedures |

**Key Procedures:**

1. **Work Planning**
   - Evaluate if elevated work can be eliminated (ground-level alternatives)
   - Identify fall hazards and required protection before work begins
   - Complete elevated work permit for non-routine tasks above 6 feet

2. **Guardrail Requirements**
   - Top rail: 42 inches (+/- 3 inches) above walking surface
   - Mid rail: Halfway between top rail and surface
   - Toe board: 4 inches minimum height where tools/materials may fall
   - Load rating: 200 lbs applied in any direction

3. **Personal Fall Arrest System (PFAS) Requirements**
   - Full-body harness required (body belts prohibited for fall arrest)
   - Anchor points rated for 5,000 lbs per attached employee
   - Free fall limited to 6 feet maximum
   - Total fall distance calculated before work begins
   - Self-retracting lifelines (SRLs) preferred for maintenance work

4. **Ladder Safety**
   - Inspect ladder before each use; tag out defective ladders
   - Maintain 3-point contact at all times
   - Do not carry tools/materials while climbing; use tool belt or hoist
   - Extend ladder 3 feet above landing surface
   - Secure ladder at top or have spotter at base
   - Do not use metal ladders near electrical hazards

5. **Portable Scaffold and Aerial Lift Safety**
   - Only trained personnel may erect scaffolds or operate aerial lifts
   - Scaffold must be on firm, level surface with locking casters
   - Guardrails required on all open sides above 4 feet
   - Aerial lift: Harness attached to boom or basket anchor, not structure
   - Do not exceed rated capacity

6. **Floor Openings and Holes**
   - All floor openings must be guarded or covered
   - Covers must be marked "HOLE" or "COVER"
   - Covers must support 2x the maximum intended load
   - Covers must be secured against displacement

7. **Rescue Planning**
   - Rescue plan required before using personal fall arrest systems
   - Self-rescue, assisted rescue, or mechanical rescue method identified
   - Rescue equipment available on-site (retrieval system, rescue ladder)
   - Suspension trauma response: rescue within 15 minutes

**Equipment Inspection Requirements:**

| Equipment | Inspection Frequency | Performed By | Action if Defective |
|-----------|---------------------|--------------|--------------------|
| Full-body harness | Before each use | User | Remove from service; notify EHS |
| Lanyards / SRLs | Before each use | User | Remove from service; notify EHS |
| Anchorage connectors | Before each use | User | Remove from service; notify EHS |
| Fixed ladders | Monthly | Maintenance | Repair or barricade |
| Portable ladders | Before each use | User | Tag out; remove from area |
| Guardrails | Weekly (production areas) | Supervisor | Repair immediately |
| Aerial lifts | Before each use | Operator | Do not operate; notify Maintenance |

**Post-Fall Procedures:**
1. Any fall arrest equipment involved in a fall must be removed from service
2. Equipment must be inspected by manufacturer or competent person before reuse
3. Incident investigation required for all falls and arrested falls
4. Medical evaluation required for any employee who has experienced fall arrest

## 6. Hazard Identification and Risk Assessment

### 6.1 Hazard Identification Methods

| Method | Description | Frequency | Responsibility |
|--------|-------------|-----------|----------------|
| Job Hazard Analysis (JHA) | Task-based hazard identification | New tasks, changes | EHS + Supervisor |
| Pre-Shift Inspections | Equipment and area checks | Daily | Operators |
| Safety Observations | Behavioral and condition observations | Ongoing | All employees |
| Near-Miss Reports | Incidents without injury | Per event | All employees |
| Formal Audits | Comprehensive program audits | Weekly/Monthly | EHS |
| Change Management | Hazard review for changes | Per change | EHS + Engineering |

### 6.2 Risk Assessment Procedure

1. **Identify the hazard** — What can cause harm?
2. **Identify who is at risk** — Operators, maintenance, visitors?
3. **Evaluate the risk** — Use severity × likelihood matrix
4. **Determine controls** — Hierarchy: Elimination → Substitution → Engineering → Administrative → PPE
5. **Implement controls** — Assign responsibility and target date
6. **Review effectiveness** — Verify controls are working

### 6.3 Risk Matrix

| Likelihood → | Rare (1) | Unlikely (2) | Possible (3) | Likely (4) | Almost Certain (5) |
|--------------|----------|--------------|--------------|------------|-------------------|
| **Catastrophic (5)** | Medium | High | High | Critical | Critical |
| **Major (4)** | Medium | Medium | High | High | Critical |
| **Moderate (3)** | Low | Medium | Medium | High | High |
| **Minor (2)** | Low | Low | Medium | Medium | High |
| **Negligible (1)** | Low | Low | Low | Medium | Medium |

**Risk Response:**
| Risk Level | Response |
|------------|----------|
| Critical | Stop work; immediate corrective action required |
| High | Implement controls within 24 hours |
| Medium | Implement controls within 7 days |
| Low | Address through normal planning process |

## 7. Incident Reporting and Investigation

### 7.1 Incident Classification

| Category | Definition | Examples |
|----------|------------|----------|
| First Aid | Treatment on-site, no medical provider | Bandage, ice pack, eye wash |
| Recordable | Medical treatment beyond first aid | Sutures, prescription medication |
| Lost Time | Inability to work following injury | Fracture, severe laceration |
| Near-Miss | Event with potential for injury/damage | Dropped load, bypassed guard |
| Property Damage | Damage to equipment or facility | Forklift impact, spill |
| Environmental | Release to air, water, or ground | Chemical spill, discharge |

### 7.2 Reporting Procedure

| Step | Action | Timeframe | Responsibility |
|------|--------|-----------|----------------|
| 1 | Ensure safety of area and provide first aid | Immediately | Witness / Supervisor |
| 2 | Notify Supervisor | Immediately | Injured party / Witness |
| 3 | Notify EHS | Within 1 hour for injuries; same shift for near-miss | Supervisor |
| 4 | Complete initial incident report | Before end of shift | Supervisor |
| 5 | Conduct investigation | Within 24 hours | EHS + Supervisor |
| 6 | Identify root causes | Within 48 hours | Investigation team |
| 7 | Assign corrective actions | Within 72 hours | EHS Manager |
| 8 | Close investigation report | Upon action completion | EHS Manager |

### 7.3 Investigation Procedure

1. Secure the scene (unless doing so creates additional hazard)
2. Provide medical attention as needed
3. Identify and interview witnesses
4. Document physical evidence (photos, measurements, samples)
5. Review relevant procedures, training records, and equipment history
6. Conduct root cause analysis (5 Whys, fishbone diagram)
7. Identify corrective and preventive actions
8. Assign actions with owners and due dates
9. Track actions to completion
10. Verify effectiveness of implemented actions
11. Share lessons learned with affected personnel

### 7.4 Regulatory Reporting

| Event Type | Reporting Requirement | Timeframe |
|------------|----------------------|-----------|
| Fatality | OSHA notification | Within 8 hours |
| In-patient hospitalization | OSHA notification | Within 24 hours |
| Amputation | OSHA notification | Within 24 hours |
| Loss of eye | OSHA notification | Within 24 hours |
| Recordable injury | OSHA 300 log entry | Within 7 days |

## 10. Emergency Preparedness

### 10.1 Emergency Types

| Emergency | Response Plan |
|-----------|---------------|
| Fire | Evacuate, assemble, account for personnel |
| Medical | First aid, 911, meet emergency responders |
| Chemical Spill | Isolate, notify EHS, contain if trained |
| Severe Weather | Shelter in designated safe areas |
| Utility Failure | Secure equipment, evacuate if necessary |
| Active Threat | Run-Hide-Fight protocol |
| Bomb Threat | Evacuate, do not use radios/cell phones |

### 10.2 Emergency Response Procedure

**Fire:**
1. Activate nearest fire alarm pull station
2. Evacuate via nearest safe exit
3. Do not use elevators
4. Proceed to designated assembly area
5. Report to Supervisor for headcount
6. Do not re-enter until "all clear" announced

**Medical Emergency:**
1. Assess scene safety
2. Call for help / activate emergency response
3. Provide first aid if trained and safe to do so
4. Call 911 for serious injuries
5. Send someone to meet emergency responders
6. Notify Supervisor and EHS

**Chemical Spill:**
1. Evacuate immediate area if not trained in spill response
2. Alert others in the area
3. Notify Supervisor and EHS immediately
4. If trained and safe, contain spill with appropriate materials
5. Reference SDS for specific response requirements
6. Do not wash spills to drain without EHS approval

## 12. Stop Work Authority

### 12.1 Policy Statement

**All employees, contractors, and visitors have the right and responsibility to stop work when they believe conditions are unsafe.** No individual will face retaliation for exercising stop work authority in good faith.

### 12.2 When to Stop Work

Stop work when you observe:
- Imminent danger to yourself or others
- Conditions that could result in serious injury
- Missing or bypassed safety devices
- Procedures not being followed
- Uncertainty about the safe way to proceed
- Equipment malfunction creating hazard

### 12.3 Stop Work Procedure

| Step | Action | Responsibility |
|------|--------|----------------|
| 1 | Stop the task or operation | Any employee |
| 2 | Move to safe location | All affected personnel |
| 3 | Notify Supervisor immediately | Person stopping work |
| 4 | Explain the concern | Person stopping work |
| 5 | Assess the situation | Supervisor + EHS if needed |
| 6 | Determine corrective action | Supervisor + EHS |
| 7 | Implement correction and verify | Supervisor |
| 8 | Resume work only when safe | Supervisor authorization |
| 9 | Document stop work event | Supervisor |
